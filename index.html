Your organisation already has
more experience than it can easily reuse.

Engineering work generates information everywhere — in reports, spreadsheets, service records, photos, machine logs, emails, project documents and, most importantly, in people's experience. Much of it is fragmented. Some of it is incomplete. Different people record different things in different ways. That does not make the information useless. The challenge is to connect the pieces that matter, understand what they actually support, recognise what is missing, and reuse relevant experience when a new problem occurs. PCTeoh Learning Lab explores how Industry Oriented Learning (IOL) and Action-to-Performance (A2P) can make this possible without requiring organisations to turn everyday work into paperwork.

How Experience Becomes Reusable

Organisations already contain enormous amounts of knowledge.

The problem is that it is rarely stored as a neat body of knowledge. A useful piece of experience may be a service report, a measurement, a photograph, a machine alarm, a project decision or an engineer's explanation. Each piece may be incomplete. The opportunity is not to make every record perfect. It is to connect relevant evidence and understand what can — and cannot — be concluded from it.

Where AI Fits

AI can connect evidence. It should not invent certainty.

Workplace information is rarely organised for the question someone will ask tomorrow. AI can help search across different sources, identify relevant relationships, compare previous cases, surface supporting evidence and highlight missing or contradictory information. But retrieval is not intelligence. A useful system must distinguish between what the evidence establishes, what the evidence suggests, and what remains uncertain. The final judgement remains with the people responsible for the work.

For Organisations

Turn Distributed Experience
into Reusable Organisational Knowledge.

Your organisation does not have one knowledge system. It has many. Customer records. Service reports. Project files. Machine data. Photos. Spreadsheets. Drawings. Emails. Procedures. And the experience of the people doing the work. They will not all use the same format. They will not all be complete. They were never created to form a single knowledge base. They do not need to be.

The opportunity is to identify the evidence that matters, connect related pieces, establish what can reasonably be concluded, and preserve the resulting experience for future work. This is where IOL and A2P meet organisational knowledge. The goal is not more documentation. The goal is better reuse of the information you already generate.

For Learning Institutions

Connecting Learning
with the Work That Follows.

Formal education provides knowledge and foundations. Professional capability develops when that knowledge is applied to real situations, decisions and consequences. IOL helps institutions connect learning with the workplace by using real work as a source of evidence, reflection and capability development.

The objective is not to turn industry into another classroom. It is to help learners become better at learning from the work they will actually do.

The A2P Framework

Action to Performance — From Experience to Reuse

A2P does not attempt to capture everything that happens at work. It focuses on the relationship that matters: what happened → what was done → what happened afterwards. This creates a practical evidence trail from which people and AI can investigate, learn and improve.

01
Event
Something happens.
02
Evidence
Capture what is available.
03
Analysis
Examine the evidence and the gap.
04
Action
Decide what to do.
05
Performance
Observe what happened afterwards.
06
Learning
What has been understood or changed?
07
Reuse
Can this experience help with a future problem?
We do not design curriculum for work. We design work that develops capability. And we preserve the experience that makes future work better.

Why This Exists

After working across industry and higher education, I became interested in a recurring problem: organisations generate enormous amounts of useful experience, but that experience is rarely organised around the problems people will need to solve in the future.

Information is distributed across systems, documents, projects and people. It is often incomplete. It is often inconsistent. And much of it was never intended to become organisational knowledge.

Yet within those fragments are clues to how problems were understood, what actions were taken, and what happened afterwards. PCTeoh Learning Lab explores how we can make those connections more visible and useful — without asking people to document everything. The aim is simple: help organisations get more value from the experience they already have.

It is not about building a large company. It is about creating a sustainable way to keep contributing.
